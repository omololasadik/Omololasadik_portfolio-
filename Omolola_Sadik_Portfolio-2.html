Mandating Health Education in Tertiary Institutions: A Necessity for Student Wellbeing
Health article · Editorial · 2024

Health education refers to the process of teaching students about different aspects of health — including physical, mental, and sexual health — as well as strategies for preventing diseases and living a healthier life. Students, largely unfamiliar with the university environment, face a number of challenges: academic stress, the transition into adulthood, and newfound freedom that can lead some to engage in risky acts. Health education equips students with the knowledge and skills to make informed health decisions throughout this critical period.

Why Health Education?

Physical health education — covering nutrition, fitness, and disease prevention — helps students develop habits with long-term benefits. Mental health education is equally important: according to the WHO, stress, depression, and anxiety are prevalent among university students, often worsened by academic pressure. Health education can provide students with the tools to manage stress and seek professional help when needed.

Sexual health education is another critical component. Nigeria has the world's second highest burden of HIV/AIDS, with 40% of new cases reported in people aged 15–24. Education on consent, contraception, and STI prevention can significantly reduce the incidence of unwanted pregnancies and transmission of infections.

What Makes It So Difficult?

Curriculum overload is a concern — students already balance heavy academic requirements. Cultural resistance around topics like sexual health and mental health adds another layer. But these challenges are not without solutions. Institutions should integrate interactive workshops, peer education programs, and practical applications into health curricula rather than relying solely on lecture-based courses.

In conclusion, mandating health education in tertiary institutions is not just an academic requirement — it is an investment in the well-being and future success of students.

Health and Lifestyle: Does Organic Necessarily Mean Healthy?
Health & lifestyle article · 2024

When people hear the word "organic," they often think of natural, healthy, and wholesome food. But what does organic really mean? Organic foods are grown or produced without synthetic pesticides, artificial fertilisers, or genetic modification. You can think of organic farming as farming the way our grandparents did it — using natural methods to grow crops and raise animals.

The Health Question

Let's debunk the conception that organic is the same as natural. Organic refers to products grown without synthetic additives, whereas "natural" is a broader term implying minimal artificial ingredients. Research shows organic foods may have slightly higher levels of certain antioxidants and omega-3 fatty acids, and they often contain fewer pesticide residues.

But here's the catch: according to UC Davis Health, organic foods are not healthier per se in terms of nutrients. You get the same nutritional benefits in conventionally grown foods. Just because something is labelled "organic" does not mean it is low in calories, sugar, or fat. Think of organic sugary snacks or organic processed meat — these are still processed foods.

Making Smart Choices as a Student

Focus on whole foods first: fresh fruits, vegetables, and whole grains should be your priority. Read labels carefully — do not be deceived by marketing tricks. Consider local alternatives: many local farmers practice traditional methods that are essentially organic, even if not certified. The vegetables from your local market might be as good as imported organic options.

In conclusion, a product being branded "organic" does not automatically make it more nutritious. An adequate diet of whole foods matters more — organic or not.

Menstrual Health Matters: Managing Menstrual Health and Cramps on Campus
Health article · Student wellness · 2024

Menstrual health is a natural part of life, yet it is often surrounded by silence and stigma — making it harder for students to manage the challenges that come with menstruation. These challenges affect studies, social life, and well-being. Menstrual cramps, also known as dysmenorrhea, are prevalent among female undergraduate students, affecting their daily routines.

How Do You Manage Menstrual Cramps?

Lifestyle changes: Regular exercise releases endorphins that act as natural pain relievers. Eating a diet rich in fruits, vegetables, and whole grains provides nutrients that reduce inflammation. Stress management — through meditation, deep breathing, or walks — can also prevent symptoms from worsening.

Pain management: Over-the-counter pain relievers like ibuprofen can reduce pain and inflammation. Heat therapy — using a hot water bottle wrapped in a towel — relaxes uterine muscles. Herbal teas like chamomile or ginger may also provide relief.

Hygiene and self-care: Change sanitary products regularly, get enough rest, and give yourself time to recover. Taking a warm bath can help with relaxation and comfort.

When to Visit the Clinic

If you experience extremely severe pain, unusual heavy bleeding, irregular periods, or something just doesn't feel right — it's time to visit a health professional. These symptoms can be signs of other health conditions that need attention.

Breaking the Stigma

Menstruation is not something to be ashamed of or hidden. We can reduce stigma by talking about it more openly — with friends, family, or classmates. The more we talk about it, the more normal it becomes.

Ecotherapy: How Connecting with Nature Can Have Positive Impacts on Our Well-Being
Health & wellness article · 2024

In this fast-paced, tech-focused world, it is essential to set aside time to appreciate nature. Connecting with nature allows us to experience an almost inexplicable calm — and living in a world so reliant on digital media, we tend to forget how profoundly this connection impacts our well-being.

What is Ecotherapy?

Ecotherapy — also called "nature therapy" or "green therapy" — describes the wide range of ways in which contact with the natural environment can promote health and well-being. It takes many forms, but all involve spending time in nature and connecting with it. Ecotherapists believe humans have an innate connection to the natural world that can be harnessed to promote healing.

Why Connecting with Nature Matters

Reduces stress: Research suggests connecting with nature has positive effects on the human brain, promoting relaxation and better cognitive functioning. Even being exposed to an image of a natural landscape can lower stress levels.

Improves learning: Studies show nature enhances education outcomes by improving academic performance, focus, and engagement. As students, immersing ourselves in nature elevates mood, reduces stress, and facilitates better assimilation and reasoning.

Improves physical health: Being outdoors in nature makes people come alive both physically and mentally — from lowered stress levels to improvements in general physical health.

Speeds up recovery: A Swedish university hospital study found that patients in rooms with nature paintings were less anxious and required smaller doses of pain medication than those without.

The relationship between humans and nature is one of mutual respect and symbiotic interdependence. Connecting with nature would not only facilitate individual and collective development but also foster a greater appreciation for the natural world.

Fashion & culture article · 2024

Fashion is an art that goes beyond time and trends. It is a reflection, a medium of expression, a creative outlet, a confidence booster, and a means of empowerment. Fashion is a non-verbal medium through which we communicate information about ourselves and our personalities.

The Artistic Importance

Fashion exists as a general standard of judgment and self-evaluation. We have all heard the aphorism "First impressions are lasting" — and the few seconds we spend processing a person's fashion taste go a long way toward deciding compatibility. Through fashion, we communicate information about ourselves without saying a word. It is a visual marker of a person's lifestyle.

The Cultural Importance

Culture has a high influence on fashion, and fashion reflects the culture of a group of people. How to Write a Job Specification (A Step-by-Step Guide)
HR & business guide · 2024

A well-written job specification is the foundation for sound recruitment. It helps employers attract top talent, improve overall job satisfaction, and ensure the right person is hired for the right role.

What Is A Job Specification?

A job specification is a document that highlights the requirements, responsibilities, and qualifications for a specific job role. Think of it as a blueprint — outlining the skills, qualifications, and experience needed, along with the duties and responsibilities expected.

Why Do We Need Job Specifications?

  • Attracting the right candidates: A clear specification helps job seekers self-select, reducing irrelevant applications.
  • Evaluating candidates: Specifications offer a basis for assessing whether candidates meet requirements.
  • Onboarding new employees: They help new hires understand expectations and responsibilities from day one.
  • Setting performance goals: Clear specifications outline expected outcomes and evaluation criteria.
  • Identifying training needs: They highlight skills gaps and development areas.

How to Write One

Include job details (title, location, type), a job summary, key responsibilities, skills and qualifications, education requirements, work experience, working conditions, and any additional information like performance measurements. A well-written specification should be accurate, specific, clear, concise, free from bias, and up-to-date.

Can My Employee See My Wage Stream? (Here's What You Should Know)
HR & workplace article · 2024

The issue of wage transparency depends on company policies, local laws, and cultural norms. In most cases, employees cannot directly access wage information without proper authorization. However, some organisations now embrace more open-pay policies to promote fairness and reduce pay gaps.

What is Wage Transparency?

Wage transparency refers to open communication about salaries and pay within an organisation. The degree of transparency varies: some companies share salary ranges for different positions, while others only disclose individual salaries. Financial privacy remains a fundamental right — laws like the Fair Credit Reporting Act regulate access to financial information in the United States.

What Should Employers Consider?

Employee morale: Open-pay policies can increase trust and motivation. When employees understand how their salaries compare, they may feel more valued. However, some may feel underpaid when learning about colleagues' earnings.

Legal considerations: In the US, the National Labour Relations Act protects employees' rights to discuss wages. Some states like Colorado, California, and New York have strict pay transparency laws. Employers must stay informed about local requirements.

Company size and culture: Smaller businesses may find open-pay systems easier to implement. Industries like technology and finance have embraced wage transparency more than others due to competitive pressures.

The bottom line: wage transparency is not a simple yes-or-no decision. Employers must carefully weigh the benefits against the challenges, ensure legal compliance, and consider their workforce's preferences before making changes.
